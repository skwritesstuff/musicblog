CHAPTER 01

Genesis & The MSK Name (Saint Ignatius, 2008–2009)

In late 2008 and early 2009, during their sophomore year at Saint Ignatius High School in Cleveland, Ohio, co-founders Myles Snider and Seamus Kelleher launched an underground music destination straight from their laptops: The MuSiK Box (themusikbox.com).

The publication’s distinct capitalization was a subtle in-joke and personal signature: The MuSiK Box cleverly embedded the co-founders' initials: Myles Snider and Seamus Kelleher (sharing the "S", with the "K" from Kelleher), spelling out MSK within MuSik using lowercase "u" and "i".

Built on early WordPress architectures and powered by DatPiff mixtape rips, Mediafire downloads, and direct email submissions from hungry indie artists, the site stood at the vanguard of the burgeoning music-blog revolution. While peers listened to top-40 radio, Seamus and Myles were tracking SoundCloud demos, underground mixtapes, and nascent producers across the country.

CHAPTER 02

Live Concerts, The Grog Shop & Mac Miller (2010)

The MuSiK Box was never just digital commentary; it was a physical live-music catalyst in the Midwest. In the spring of 2010, Seamus and Myles partnered with Cleveland promotion crew The Coventry Kids to book an 18-year-old Pittsburgh prodigy named Mac Miller at The Grog Shop in Cleveland Heights.

“Cleveland, a little fun fact for you: actually the first out-of-town headlining show I ever did was at the Grog Shop.”

— Mac Miller on The Arroyo Show (House of Blues Cleveland, Oct 10, 2011)

This pivotal show took place months before K.I.D.S. (Kickin' Incredibly Dope Shit) dropped and skyrocketed Mac to superstardom. The concert cemented the site's reputation among major indie labels, including Pittsburgh's Rostrum Records.

Just weeks earlier, on April 22, 2010, the co-founders shot a now-legendary parking lot interview on an iPhone in the Saint Ignatius High School lot with a 19-year-old Machine Gun Kelly (MGK), breaking down his breakout mixtape 100 Words and Running. The site followed this in April 2011 by co-producing the Animal House Tour at The Grog Shop featuring Kansas breakout XV and college-rap phenoms The Dean's List.

CHAPTER 03

The 301 Rebrand & IAWT 2.0 (Early 2011)

As readership expanded beyond Ohio into a national footprint, the co-founders orchestrated a comprehensive brand evolution in early 2011, executing a 301 permanent redirect from themusikbox.com (128 preserved snapshots) to inaudiowetrust.com (1,630+ preserved snapshots).

Collaborating with web designer and technical architect Evan Edwards, "IAWT 2.0" introduced a sleek, high-contrast dark aesthetic, custom audio streaming players, and categorized music channels covering Hip Hop, Indie / Rock, Electronic / Dubstep, and Mashups.

To keep up with the unrelenting volume of PR submissions and exclusive premieres, Seamus and Myles assembled an expanded editorial squad including staff writers Helen, Ben, Mike, Pilo, Lesia, and Tricia.

CHAPTER 04

The 100,000+ Viral Surge & Mashup Culture

During its peak in 2011, In Audio We Trust averaged 2,000 to 6,000+ daily readers. On record-shattering days, viral algorithmic traffic from StumbleUpon, Reddit, and Hype Machine propelled single-day visitor counts above 100,000 unique visitors.

A major engine of this explosive growth was the golden age of collegiate mashup culture. IAWT was the premier launchpad for artists like The White Panda, The Hood Internet, Super Mashed Bros, Norwegian Recycling, Brenton Duvall, and Big Z Remixes, generating millions of collective streams.

CHAPTER 05

The Prescient Ear: Generational Early Finds

What made The MuSiK Box & IAWT unique in music history was their staggering track record of identifying future stadium-filling megastars months or years before mainstream recognition:

Kendrick Lamar

Featured in March 2011 (The Kendrick Lamar Experience Documentary) 4 months prior to Section.80.

Childish Gambino

Spotlighted in Feb 2011 during the Derrick Comedy / Community era, long before his debut album Camp.

The Weeknd

Covered in Sept 2011 with "The Morning" when Abel Tesfaye was completely anonymous during the House of Balloons era.

Frank Ocean

Featured in May 2011 with "Acura Integurl" and "Thinking About You" more than a year before Channel Orange.

Tyler, The Creator & OFWGKTA

Spring 2011 coverage of "Gozeraaah" and "Tron Cat" during the defining Goblin release wave.

A$AP Rocky

Championed in October 2011 upon the debut of Live. Love. A$AP.

Lana Del Rey

Featured in Dec 2011 ("Summertime Sadness Live") before the global drop of Born to Die.

Macklemore

Original media and editorial archive from August 2010 — over two full years before The Heist and "Thrift Shop".

CHAPTER 06

The Definitive Finale (January 2012)

By early 2012, as high school drew toward graduation and college loomed, the co-founders sold the publication, concluding their storied 50/50 partnership.

The authentic publication record closed in January 2012 with a poetic pair of final features: Mac Miller – Jerry's Record Store (paying homage to the artist they helped break in Cleveland two years prior) and Mike Posner – Looks Like Sex.

Following January 2012, publication ceased for a three-month blackout before external owners resumed sporadic posting. This archive strictly preserves the genuine, curated August 2009 – January 2012 era: 307 posts and 54 media artifacts representing a golden snapshot of millennial music discovery.
